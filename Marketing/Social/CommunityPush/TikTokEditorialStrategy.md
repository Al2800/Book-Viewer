# BookQuotes TikTok Editorial Strategy

Updated: 27 July 2026
Bead: `bd-tkrc`

## Channel Position

BookQuotes should be a useful book-discovery and reading-culture account that happens to make an
app. A reader should want to follow the channel even if they never download BookQuotes.

The product appears as evidence of a real reading habit: noticing passages, keeping context,
remembering books, and returning to ideas. It should not interrupt every recommendation.

## Editorial Mix

- 60% book recommendations and discovery
- 20% reader identity, discussion, annotation, and book-club prompts
- 15% BookQuotes workflow and product proof
- 5% launch, founder, and company updates

This means roughly one app-led post for every six or seven editorial posts.

## Cadence

Use two potential publishing slots each day:

- 12:30: recommendation slideshow, short list, or discussion prompt
- 19:30: Reel, deeper recommendation, trend response, or occasional app proof

One strong daily post is the minimum. Use the second slot only when the content is distinct and
passes review. During the first 14 days, aim for 10 to 14 primary posts and three to five
additional posts rather than forcing 28 pieces.

TikTok's own organic guidance prioritises quality over raw frequency. Increasing to two posts
every day should be earned by watch time, saves, shares, profile visits, and the ability to
maintain genuinely useful recommendations.

## Repeatable Series

### Read This If

Three recommendations connected by a reader need rather than a broad genre:

- Read this if you want a mystery where the place matters as much as the crime
- Read this if you want fantasy without a 100-page setup
- Read this if you want a short book that changes how you see ordinary work

### One Book, Three Reasons

A 15 to 25-second Reel or four-slide post:

1. Who the book is for
2. What makes it distinctive
3. One honest reservation
4. The reader mood in which it works best

### Backlist, Not Hype

Recommend an older title that remains worth reading. Explain why it has lasted rather than
calling it underrated without evidence.

### The Pairing

Pair two books by theme, structure, or emotional effect:

- Read these together
- The classic and its modern echo
- Same premise, completely different book

### Book-Club Fuel

Recommend a book with one specific discussion question. Avoid plot-summary captions.

### New Release Radar

Cover one to three current releases only after checking the publisher description, publication
date, format availability, and at least two credible reviews. Label advance or unread
recommendations honestly.

### Reader Debate

Annotation styles, abandoned reading habits, rereading, ratings, book-buying rules, and how
people preserve passages. These should invite a real answer, not manufacture engagement.

### BookQuotes Proof

Use the app only where it naturally solves the topic:

- keeping one line from a recommended book
- finding an old passage
- preparing three passages for book club
- building a digital commonplace book

## Recommendation Standard

Every recommendation must answer:

1. Who is this for?
2. What is distinctive about it?
3. What expectation should be set honestly?
4. Has the title, author, edition, and publication status been verified?
5. Are we recommending from direct reading, credible research, or current community interest?

Do not pretend a book has been personally read when it has not. Use language such as "on our
research list" or "readers are recommending" when appropriate.

## Visual Rights

- Prefer original photographs of legally owned physical books.
- Use publisher-supplied publicity assets only under their stated terms or with permission.
- Do not download cover images from retailer listings for marketing reuse.
- Typography-led slideshows can name books without reproducing cover art.
- Keep quoted passages short and necessary for commentary; prefer summaries and reader reactions.
- Record the source and permission status for every visual before approval.

## Two-Week Content Bank

Build the first bank around seven balanced territories:

| Territory | Primary format | Minimum pieces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read This If | Slideshow | 3 |
| One Book, Three Reasons | Reel | 2 |
| Backlist, Not Hype | Reel or slideshow | 2 |
| The Pairing | Slideshow | 2 |
| Book-Club Fuel | Slideshow | 2 |
| Reader Debate | Reel or text-led post | 2 |
| BookQuotes Proof | Reel | 2 |

Select exact titles only after checking current UK availability, visual rights, and the intended
audience mix. Avoid letting romance and fantasy dominate the opening bank: include literary
fiction, crime or thriller, nonfiction, classics or backlist, and at least one accessible wildcard.

## Review Gate

Before scheduling:

1. Verify every title, author, claim, and publication date.
2. Confirm visual rights and record the source.
3. Watch or read the complete asset on a phone.
4. Check that the opening frame makes the recommendation clear within two seconds.
5. Confirm captions are useful without the hashtags.
6. Check that the post does not imply sponsorship, publisher approval, or personal reading that
   did not occur.
7. Obtain approval before pressing Schedule or Post.

## Measurement

Compare recommendation territories separately from product posts:

- two-second and six-second hold
- completion rate
- saves and shares per 1,000 views
- comments that name a reading preference or another title
- profile visits and follows
- link taps and App Store activity on product posts

Review after seven days. Repeat formats that create saves, shares, and follows, not merely views.
