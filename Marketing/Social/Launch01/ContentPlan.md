# BookQuotes App Store Launch Content

## Positioning

BookQuotes is the reading companion for people who underline passages, highlight pages,
and write in the margins. It turns marked paper pages into a searchable personal quote
library.

Primary promise: **Keep the lines you underlined.**

Supporting proof:

- Add books by ISBN barcode or manual entry.
- Photograph a marked page and use AI-first extraction.
- Review and correct extracted text before saving.
- Search books and saved quotes from one library.
- Use on-device extraction when remote processing is unavailable.
- Export the library when needed.

Avoid claiming perfect extraction, automatic cover recognition from a photograph, cloud
sync, or completely offline AI. The v1 experience is review-first and the library is
stored locally.

## Founder Launch Note

I built BookQuotes because I kept underlining passages in paper books and then losing
them inside the books themselves.

The app lets you add a book, photograph a marked page, review the extracted quote, and
keep it in a searchable library. It is deliberately made for paper-book readers rather
than trying to replace the book.

BookQuotes is now available on iPhone and iPad. I would especially value feedback from
people who underline, highlight, or write in the margins.

## Website Editorial Plan

Publish one useful article each week and reuse its strongest idea as a carousel.

1. **How to build a digital commonplace book from paper books**
   Target: readers searching for a sustainable annotation system.
2. **What to do with book highlights after you finish reading**
   Target: readers who underline but rarely revisit their notes.
3. **Underlining, highlighting or margin notes: which method helps recall?**
   Target: practical comparison and discussion.
4. **How BookQuotes handles AI extraction and reader control**
   Target: privacy, consent, review-first workflow and fallback.
5. **A simple weekly review ritual for your saved book quotes**
   Target: retention and ongoing product use.

Each article should include real app screenshots, one specific reader problem, a useful
method that stands alone without the app, and a restrained App Store call to action.

## Zero-Budget Distribution

- Cross-post Reels to Instagram and Facebook without a platform watermark.
- Share every feed post to Stories with a poll or question, not only a link.
- Leave useful comments on reader, annotation and commonplace-book posts.
- Contact five small book creators or reading newsletters each week with a personal note.
- Offer Collab posts where a creator demonstrates their own annotation workflow.
- Ask early users for honest screenshots or short observations; do not script praise.
- Recut the Reel with a new first sentence rather than repeatedly reposting the same file.

## Measurement

Review after 7 and 14 days:

- Saves and shares per post.
- Non-follower reach.
- Reel three-second views and completion rate.
- Profile visits and App Store link taps.
- Product page views and downloads in App Store Connect.
- Trial starts and trial-to-paid conversion.

Use comments and direct messages as qualitative research. Record exact phrases readers
use to describe their annotation problem; those phrases should shape the next hooks.
