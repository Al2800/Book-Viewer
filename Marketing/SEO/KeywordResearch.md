# BookQuotes Keyword Research

Updated: 11 August 2026

## Status

This is the first evidence-led keyword pass for the BookQuotes website. It combines qualitative
search-result review, competitor language, the shipped product workflow, and likely reader questions.
It does not claim exact monthly search volume, CPC, or difficulty. Those figures require access to
Google Keyword Planner, a comparable SEO tool, or first-party Search Console data after Search
Console has accumulated enough first-party evidence.

The working rule is to prioritise search intent that maps to a real BookQuotes workflow. The site
should answer the reader's problem first and introduce the app where it genuinely helps. It should
not become a large set of near-duplicate pages built around slightly different keyword phrases.

## What The Results Tell Us

### 1. The strongest wedge is a physical-book workflow

The most concrete search need is not “read famous quotes.” It is “I marked a paper page and want
to keep the useful passage.” Competitors repeatedly describe camera capture, OCR, book context,
search, tags and export. BookQuotes should own the honest version of that workflow: capture,
review, correct, connect to the book, and revisit.

### 2. “Book quotes” is a category term, not the whole positioning

The broad phrase is crowded by public quote collections, older unrelated apps, and products with
different purposes. Use it on the product page and in supporting copy, but lead headlines with the
reader problem and the physical-page workflow. Do not create pages consisting of famous quotations
or scraped book text.

### 3. Privacy can differentiate, but only with precise language

Competitors use “private,” “offline,” and “on-device” prominently. BookQuotes can discuss local
library storage and the separate remote AI route, but must not use an absolute “nothing leaves your
phone” claim while remote processing exists. The privacy guide should link directly to the current
policy and explain deletion, export, catalogue lookup, and remote processing boundaries.

### 4. Commonplace-book language is useful for discovery, not the only conversion route

“Digital commonplace book” attracts readers interested in reflection, annotation and idea reuse.
It is a good editorial entry point, but the page should explain the practice before presenting the
app. The conversion path is strongest when it connects the practice to a specific capture and
retrieval habit.

## Competitor And SERP Evidence

- [Bookly: How to save quotes from books](https://blog.getbookly.com/save-quotes-using-bookly/) demonstrates a long-form how-to page around photographing, cropping, processing and editing passages.
- [QuoteCache](https://www.quotecache.com/) uses “book quote app,” “save quotes from books,” on-device OCR, private library and offline-first language.
- [BookLeaf](https://bookleaf.mjo.app/) uses “scan book pages into searchable reading notes,” with book, tag and OCR language.
- [QuoteMark on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/quotemark-ai-scan-book-quotes/id6471570608) uses “AI Scan Book Quotes,” book categories, tags, export and digital commonplace-book positioning.
- [Citez](https://citezapp.com/) frames the category around saving quotes and learnings, tags, quote libraries and prompts.
- [GoodForms](https://goodforms.app/) validates “digital commonplace book” as a reader-facing concept, while keeping the product broader than book quotes.
- [MindNest on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mindnest.app) shows newer competition around book OCR, AI quote search and personal knowledge management.
- [Google's people-first guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) warns against mass-produced pages, extensive automation, trend-chasing and content written mainly to attract search traffic.

## Content Rules

- One substantial page per search intent, not one page per synonym.
- Answer the query in the first section.
- Use screenshots from the shipped app and describe limitations honestly.
- Keep the reader's own judgement central; extraction is not a guarantee.
- Do not imply firsthand reading of books used in social or research content.
- Do not publish substantial copyrighted passages or build a public quote database.
- Link to the App Store with a distinct `utm_source=organic&utm_medium=website` campaign.
- Link guides to the privacy policy, support page, and related workflow pages.

## Measurement Plan

The domain is live, but the following first-party evidence remains a separate operational step:

1. Verify the domain in Google Search Console and submit `/sitemap.xml` only through the approved `.8.1` workflow.
2. Group queries into capture, organisation, commonplace, privacy and branded buckets.
3. Record impressions, clicks, CTR and average position by page and bucket.
4. Add App Store links with UTM parameters and compare qualified visits, installs and trial starts where attribution is available.
5. Refresh pages only when the change improves the answer, evidence, screenshots or internal links.

The first 30-day decision rule is simple: keep pages that attract relevant impressions or App Store
visits, improve pages that receive impressions but weak clicks, and avoid expanding a cluster that
has no evidence of reader interest.
